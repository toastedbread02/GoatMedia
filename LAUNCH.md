# Sharing GoatMedia

Copy that is ready to paste, plus the things that actually decide whether a post gets views.

**The link:** https://toastedbread02.github.io/GoatMedia/

## What to say about it

The one detail worth leading with is the hand review. It is unusual, it is true, and it
explains why the feed is small — which people read as honest rather than empty.

> Every video on here is watched by a person before it goes live. No autoplay farm, no
> algorithm dumping garbage into the feed.

Avoid describing it as "a TikTok for goats". It invites comparisons the site does not need
and makes people expect a feed that is already full.

## Reddit

Post the link only in communities where a person could plausibly want it, and read the
rules first — many subreddits remove self-promotion on sight, and a removal is not
recoverable by posting again.

**Title options**

- I built a video site for goats, and every upload gets watched by a human before it goes live
- Goat videos, hand-reviewed: a small site I made because the big platforms bury them
- I made a place for goat videos that isn't a hashtag on someone else's app

**Body**

```
I keep goats and I got tired of goat clips being scattered across platforms that don't
care about them, so I built GoatMedia.

It works like a normal video feed — vertical scrolling, likes, comments, following,
sound — but with one deliberate difference: every single upload is queued and watched by
a person before it appears. Nothing is auto-approved, so the feed stays small and stays
goats.

It also has photo posts with albums and borrowed sounds, a recommendation feed that
explains why it showed you something, and a moderation dashboard for the review queue.

No account needed to watch: https://toastedbread02.github.io/GoatMedia/

If you have a goat clip, upload it — I review the queue myself, usually within a day.
Happy to hear what's broken.
```

Subreddits worth trying, in rough order of fit: `r/goats`, `r/goatsofinstagram` (if the
rules allow links), `r/homestead`, `r/farming`, `r/selfhosted` or `r/SideProject` for the
build story rather than the goats. Read each sidebar; several require a minimum account
age or ban link posts entirely.

## X / Bluesky / Mastodon

no autoplay slop, no algorithm guessing.

Vertical feed, photo albums, borrowed sounds, and a recommendation feed that tells you
why it picked something.

https://toastedbread02.github.io/GoatMedia/

A second post the next day, with one actual clip attached, does better than repeating the
link. Clip-first posts get watched; link-first posts get scrolled past.

## Discord, WhatsApp, group chats

```
made a goat video site, hand-reviewed uploads only — https://toastedbread02.github.io/GoatMedia/
```

Nobody clicks a paragraph in a group chat. One line, one link.

## What actually moves the numbers

- **Upload something first.** A feed with three real posts converts far better than an
  empty one, and the review queue needs material.
- **Post a clip natively, then mention the site in a comment.** Reddit and X both punish
  bare links and reward media.
- **Answer every comment.** Early on, replies are the only thing that makes the place feel
  alive.
- **Ask for one specific thing.** "Tell me if the upload flow breaks on your phone" gets
  replies; "feedback welcome" does not.
- **Share the build story somewhere technical.** The moderation queue, the
  recommendation weights and the in-browser 1080p compression are genuinely interesting
  to developers, and that audience is more likely to make an account.

## What not to do

- Do not buy views, followers or engagement. The recommendation engine reads real watch
  time and completion, so bought numbers make the feed worse, not better.
- Do not post the same link in ten subreddits on the same day. It reads as spam and gets
  accounts banned.
- Do not claim user numbers. There is no need to, and it is the fastest way to lose the
  one thing the site has going for it: it tells the truth.
